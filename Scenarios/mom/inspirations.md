# Inspirations — MoM Total Conversion

## The Color Wheel (MtG + Enneagram)

The five spheres form a wheel of opposition. Each sphere has a NATURAL ENEMY
(the thing that destroys it) and a NATURAL ALLY (the thing it complements).
This is the Enneagram's contraposition principle applied to game design:
each position's greatest weakness is the thing its opposite does effortlessly.

```
              LIFE (White)
             /    \
      NATURE       SORCERY
      (Green)      (Blue)
             \    /
              \/
       CHAOS --- DEATH
       (Red)     (Black)
```

### Enemy Pairs (immunity axis)

| Sphere | Natural Enemy | Why |
|--------|--------------|-----|
| Life | Death | Creation vs destruction; healing is the opposite of decay |
| Death | Life | Purification burns undead; light banishes shadow |
| Nature | Sorcery | Raw force vs intellect; you can't outsmart a tidal wave |
| Sorcery | Nature | Control vs primal chaos; structure crumbles against wild growth |
| Chaos | Sorcery (primary) | Disorder vs order; but chaos also disrupts itself |

### Sphere Identities

| Sphere | Theme | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|-------|----------|----------|
| Life | Protection, healing, angels | Shields allies, purifies undead | Low offense, slow |
| Nature | Growth, creatures, force | Massive creatures, raw stats | Vulnerable to control |
| Sorcery | Knowledge, illusion, control | Dispel, counter, manipulation | Fragile creatures |
| Death | Undead, drain, inevitability | Ignores conventional defense | Stopped cold by holy |
| Chaos | Fire, randomness, destruction | Highest burst damage, unpredictable | Self-destructive, unreliable |

### Resistance Principles

1. **Opposing sphere immunity.** Your natural enemy's creatures are IMMUNE to your
   signature spells. A Death Knight's Death Wish bounces off a Paladin. A Storm
   Drake's Stasis bounces off a Behemoth. This is absolute — no roll, no save.

2. **Aura protection.** Common units of the opposing sphere grant +30% resistance
   to co-located allies. A Guardian Spirit next to your army gives everyone a 30%
   chance to resist Death magic. This IS a roll — not guaranteed.

3. **Chaos entropy.** Chaos creatures have a flat 20% resistance to ALL targeted
   spells. Chaos doesn't have a specific counter — it's just inherently hard to
   pin down. But it also can't be protected the same way (no single aura helps).

4. **Heroes amplify.** Heroes of a sphere serve as mobile aura emitters for their
   sphere's protection type. Ariel (Life) protects against Death. Freya (Nature)
   protects against Sorcery. Tauron (Chaos) protects against Nature.

5. **Artifacts transcend sphere.** The Lamp grants flat +25% resistance regardless
   of sphere matchup. Future artifacts will be sphere-typed for specific protections.

### MtG Parallels

- Protection from [color] → our immunity to opposing sphere's spells
- Hexproof → our hero self-resistance (50%)
- Ward → our artifact protection (pay extra or it bounces)
- Indestructible → our undead-vs-death (can't kill what's already dead)
- First Strike → our positioning requirement (you must GET there first)

### Enneagram Dynamics

Each sphere's growth direction (what it NEEDS from its ally):
- Life needs Nature's growth to sustain itself (healing without growth stagnates)
- Nature needs Life's protection to survive long enough to reach full power
- Sorcery needs Death's inevitability to close games it controls
- Death needs Chaos's aggression to break through Life's shields
- Chaos needs Sorcery's direction to focus its power (unfocused chaos is just noise)

Each sphere's stress direction (what it becomes under pressure):
- Life under pressure becomes rigid (over-protection, turtling)
- Nature under pressure becomes mindless (all creatures, no strategy)
- Sorcery under pressure becomes paralyzed (too many options, never commits)
- Death under pressure becomes desperate (over-extends drain, goes insolvent)
- Chaos under pressure becomes self-destructive (friendly fire, burnout)

### Design Implication for Gameplay

The player's ARMY COMPOSITION is their spell resistance posture. If you're playing
Death and you see Life creatures incoming, you know your kill spells are useless —
you need your Chaos ally to break through with fire instead. If you're Nature and
Sorcery is controlling your creatures, you need raw stat monsters that can't be
dispelled (Behemoth doesn't care about your illusions).

This creates a meta-game of scouting and counter-building that mirrors MtG's
sideboard strategy: you see what your opponent is doing and adapt your army.

## The Chess Analogy (positioning as permission)

Three axes of the magic system make it chess-like:

1. **Piece identity** — which unit you have determines which spells are available
2. **Piece position** — range from caster to target gates whether the spell can fire
3. **Piece defense** — target composition determines whether the spell lands

A Death Knight approaching an unprotected city is a queen bearing down on a king.
Add a Paladin to the garrison and the Death Knight becomes a pawn — its signature
move is neutralized by the defender's positioning.

## The TRIZ Principles In Use

- **Asymmetry (P4):** Resistance isn't symmetric. Life resists Death, Death does NOT resist Life.
- **Counterweight (P8):** Each spell has a specific counter-unit, not a generic "magic shield."
- **Prior Action (P10):** Protection must be positioned BEFORE the spell lands.
- **Nesting (P7):** Auras nest — a Paladin protects the whole stack, not just itself.
- **Segmentation (P1):** Different protections for different schools. No universal shield.
- **Universality (P6):** The resistance check is ONE system with per-sphere parameters (not 5 separate systems).
